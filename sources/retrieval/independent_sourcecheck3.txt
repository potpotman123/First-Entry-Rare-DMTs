## Opportunities exist in small markets

TKT is developing iduronate-2-sulphatase (I2S) to treat Hunter’s syndrome (MPS-II), which is caused by an X-linked deficiency of I2S. Patients suffering from the disorder manifest similar symptoms to MPS-I patients, but with differing severities. TKT initiated the pivotal trial of I2S in 2003 and expects to file for marketing approval in early 2005. Nearly all 2,000 patients with Hunter’s syndrome have been diagnosed, thereby facilitating marketing efforts. TKT will seek to commercialize I2S by building a US sales force and will also employ its European sales force. TKT has partnered with Genzyme to leverage its strong commercialization expertise in Japan.

BioMarin’s Aryplase has recently entered Phase III testing to treat Maroteaux–Lamy syndrome (MPS-VI), an autosomal recessive disease. Patients are typically diagnosed in the first six to twenty-four months of life and die of cardiomyopathy in their teens. BioMarin expects to file for marketing approval by year-end 2004. Although Aryplase will probably yield encouraging clinical results, the small market size might translate into a challenging commercialization effort. BioMarin intends to seek a partner for ex-US distribution of the drug. In the United States, the company will probably lead the commercialization efforts. ▶
