●  Patient population: The study will initially enroll a sentinel
  patient aged 8–10 years before expanding to patients as young
  as 2–3 years, where earlier intervention could improve signal
  detection by enabling treatment before substantial neurological
  impairment occurs.


●  Study design: Placebo-controlled MAD design with 3 cohorts.
  Management indicated that the study design is adaptive, which
  we believe would help shift enrollment towards the dose with the
  best efficacy/safety profile in pediatric patients. We look forward
  to additional details later today.


      ○  Given CMP-002’s PK/PD profile is broadly consistent with
         other oligonucleotides, management believes the NHP data
         provide a reasonable basis for anticipating translation to
         humans.


      ○  Management believes the placebo-controlled design
         could facilitate a registrational path and potentially support
         accelerated/conditional approval.



●  Endpoints: The company is including efficacy assessments
  in the Phase 1/2 study across all major disease domains,
  including seizures, sleep, motor, communication, behavior and
  development.


      ○  Early data from ProMMiS validated a protocol with a full
         battery of assessments that is feasible in SRD patients.


      ○  Seizures: Countable seizures appear to be present in
         approximately 20%–45% of SRD patients aged 2–10 years
         (according to ProMMiS); therefore, we will be watching for
         further details on CAMP’s planned approach to seizure
         assessment.


      ○  Cognition and neurodevelopmental endpoints: The
         company is planning to measure Bayley, Vineland and
         GMFM-66 with early ProMMis data demonstrating no
         significant floor or ceiling effects with the raw scores to date.


      ○  Sleep: The company is planning to use EEG and video
         EEG.


      ○  Patient-reported outcomes and diaries by incorporating
         input from patient organizations.


      ○  Timelines: Importantly, assessment timelines are expected
         to vary across the different outcomes, and today’s update
         should provide greater clarity on this important aspect of the
         study design.



●  Cash position: As of end of June, CAMP had ~$130M in
  proforma cash extending runway through the end of 2028.

INVESTMENT THESIS




●  SRD represents a high-unmet-need genetic disorder driven by SYNGAP1
  haploinsufficiency, with no approved disease-modifying therapies and current treatment
  limited to symptomatic management.


●  CMP-002 offers a compelling disease-modifying approach by targeting regRNA to
  increase endogenous SYNGAP1 expression and restore protein levels; importantly, the
  program is currently leading the competitive development landscape.


●  CMP-002 leverages an established CNS therapeutic modality, with intrathecal ASO
  delivery clinically validated by approved therapies including Spinraza and Qalsody, helping
  reduce modality and delivery risk.


●  Beyond CMP-002, we see meaningful unmodeled upside from potential BD/licensing
  of CMP-001 in UCD and additional programs generated through CAMP's proprietary
  RAP platform, which is very versatile. We currently ascribe no explicit value to these
  opportunities despite clinical data already generated for CMP-001.

VALUATION

Our $14 PT is based on a SOTP analysis that forecasts cash flows into the early 2040s, with
a 12% discount rate.
RISKS TO VALUATION


●  Disease heterogeneity could make it challenging to demonstrate a clear and consistent
  efficacy signal across patients.


●  Clinical benefit could be largely confined to seizure reduction, with limited improvement in
  more meaningful behavioral and cognitive outcomes, potentially constraining adoption.


●  Unmodeled competitive entrants, including potentially disruptive therapeutic modalities,
  could reduce CMP-002’s addressable market and commercial opportunity.


●  Unexpected safety findings, particularly those that limit dosing or chronic administration,
  could negatively impact the program’s development and commercial potential.
