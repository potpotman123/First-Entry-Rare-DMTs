Additional details on upcoming catalysts:

●  Phase 1 data of SRRK’s SRK-439 in healthy volunteers by year-end to assess
  safety and tolerability profile of SRK-439 and PK/PD profile. Note that SRK-439
  is more potent than apitegromab and it's been optimized for subcutaneous
  formulation.


●  CHMP opinion on apitegromab in SMA is now expected in 1H27 following
  resubmission of the MAA earlier this week (see our note HERE). We had
  anticipated that FDA approval would provide meaningful validation of the alternate
  fill-finish facility, further strengthening our confidence in a positive CHMP opinion in
  1H27.


●  Clarity on the pivotal trial design for BNTC’s BB-301 in OPMD is now
  expected in 1Q27 (vs. prior guidance of 3Q26/4Q26), following an FDA request
  for 12-month natural history data in addition to the previously submitted 6-month
  dataset. The company expects to provide an update following FDA alignment on
  the proposed design.


●  LRMR remains on track to complete its rolling BLA submission for
  nomlabofusp in FA in 2H26, seeking accelerated approval based on skin
  FXN levels. We see a credible path to approval given encouraging efficacy data,
  CMC readiness, and constructive FDA engagement, although the safety database
  remains the key risk and could modestly delay review (LINK).


●  Novartis (NVS, Not Rated)/PTCT plan to engage with the FDA to discuss the
  emerging clinical data for votoplam, including the 24-month results from
  PIVOT-HD trial demonstrating ~50% slowing of disease progression versus
  matched ENROLL-HD natural history controls. That said, our base case
  conservatively assumes a Phase 3 trial will be required for approval. Importantly,
  the ongoing Phase 3 INVEST-HD trial incorporates key learnings from Phase 2
  PIVOT-HD, particularly around patient selection, which we believe increases the
  likelihood of clinical success.


●  PTCT remains on track to complete the rolling BLA submission for
  accelerated approval of the recently acquired ST-920 from Sangamo
  (SGMOQ, Not Rated) in 4Q26.


●  RYTM expects to launch setmelanotide in aHO in Japan by year-end 2026.
  We view Japan as a meaningful commercial opportunity, with an estimated 5,000–
  8,000 patients and potentially favorable pricing expected to fall between U.S. and
  European levels.


●  RYTM is poised to begin a country-by-country rollout of setmelanotide
  across Europe for setmelanotide in aHO. Note that the EU opportunity is
  significant with a population size ranging from 8,000-10,000 patients.

BNTC INVESTMENT THESIS

Our investment thesis is four-fold:

●  There is a clear unmet need in OPMD with no approved disease-modifying therapy to
  date, and no competition and only limited symptomatic options for dysphagia, a major
  driver of poor quality of life and mortality.


●  We believe BB-301's differentiated "silence-and-replace" strategy is well suited to address
  the underlying pathophysiology of OPMD with the potential to provide durable therapeutic
  benefit.


●  There are ~3,000 patients in the US and ~15,000 patients worldwide, with a strong founder
  effect leading to concentrations of patients in Quebec, Canada, among Bukhara Jews and
  at high-volume centers including NYU and UNM.


●  The company is aiming to obtain FDA alignment on the Phase 3 study design, including
  a company-sponsored natural history lead-in period within a single-arm design and an
  endpoint strategy incorporating both objective and patient-reported outcomes.

VALUATION

Our $18 PT is based on an SOTP analysis that forecasts cash flows into the late 2040s, with
a 12% discount rate.

RISKS TO VALUATION

Risks to our outlook and valuation include:

●  Setbacks in the clinical development of BNTC's only program, BB-301,


●  Safety signals in the clinical development of BB-301 that would deter commercial adoption,


●  Competitive threats in the gene therapy and RNAi spaces from other emerging biotech
  companies, and


●  Greater-than-expected operating expenses and cash burn, resulting in equity issuances
  more frequently or at a less attractive valuation than we project.

CAMP INVESTMENT THESIS




●  SRD represents a high-unmet-need genetic disorder driven by SYNGAP1
  haploinsufficiency, with no approved disease-modifying therapies and current treatment
  limited to symptomatic management.


●  CMP-002 offers a compelling disease-modifying approach by targeting regRNA to
  increase endogenous SYNGAP1 expression and restore protein levels; importantly, the
  program is currently leading the competitive development landscape.

LRMR INVESTMENT THESIS

1 The company's only product, Nomlabofusp, is differentiated by directly addressing the
  underlying FXN deficiency in FA, delivering mature FXN to mitochondria and restoring
  levels toward those seen in asymptomatic carriers. In contrast, Skyclarys acts downstream
  of the core defect, leaving substantial unmet need for an upstream disease-modifying
  therapy.
2 In our view, we believe nomlabofusp’s benefit-risk profile is favorable, with sustained FXN
  restoration through 18 months, encouraging and potentially deepening clinical benefit
  across mFARS and other functional measures, and supportive long-term exposure data
  at the 50 mg dose. While anaphylaxis remains the key safety risk and could necessitate a
  boxed warning and/or REMS upon approval, potentially limiting adoption, we believe the
  risk is manageable through modified dosing and mitigation strategies and is unlikely to
  meaningfully constrain use given the high unmet need and encouraging efficacy profile.
3 We believe the current BLA package has a credible path to accelerated approval,
  supported by FDA alignment on skin FXN as a surrogate endpoint (pending review),
  encouraging functional data, a well-advanced CMC package, and continued constructive
  agency engagement. The key remaining risk is the safety database, which could modestly
  delay review if additional follow-up is required, although management believes this could

PTCT INVESTMENT THESIS
1 We believe Sephience can sustain long-term top-line growth and offset declines in the
  legacy DMD franchise, supported by its differentiated clinical profile, encouraging U.S.

2 We view votoplam as a compelling HD opportunity, supported by robust mHTT lowering,
  an encouraging dose-dependent cUHDRS trend, and a favorable safety and convenience

  the program’s likelihood of success.
3 We see meaningful medium-term pipeline optionality across multiple programs, including
  vatiquinone in Friedreich's ataxia (FA), oral small-molecule programs targeting NLRP3 in
  pulmonary inflammatory diseases, second-gen DHODH, as well as gene therapy in Fabry
  disease. Importantly, PTC continues to leverage its expertise in oral small-molecule and
  splicing platforms targeting MSH3 (to stall CAG expansion in HD in a manner upstream
  of votoplam’s MoA) and ATXN3 to expand into several high-unmet-need indications. We
  assign a 75% POS to vatiquinone in FA, with limited value currently ascribed to NLRP3
  and Fabry, leaving upside as these programs are further de-risked.

RYTM INVESTMENT THESIS
1 Rhythm's differentiated MC4R agonist franchise addresses significant unmet
  need across rare obesity disorders driven by impaired MC4R pathway signaling
  and requiring specialized management distinct from general obesity care. Its
  first-generation product, setmelanotide, is approved in Bardet-Biedl syndrome and
  several biallelic monogenic obesity indications and was recently approved in acquired
  hypothalamic obesity (aHO), a meaningful opportunity encompassing ~25,000 patients
  across the U.S., Europe, and Japan.
2 We are positive on the aHO opportunity given the significant unmet need and
  limitations of current treatment options. Off-label GLP-1 receptor agonists benefit some
  patients but primarily address weight, whereas MC4R agonists can improve both weight
  and hyperphagia. We model >$2B in peak sales for aHO, incremental to +$500M from the
  previously approved monogenic obesity indications. Early launch trends are encouraging,
  with management executing well on market access, and we expect strong U.S. momentum
  to continue into 2027 alongside the ex-U.S. rollout beginning YE 2026. Rhythm is also
  developing second-gen assets with improved safety and convenience that could enhance
  persistence and drive upside to our estimates.
3 We are optimistic about the potential expansion into Prader–Willi syndrome (PWS),
  supported by a strong biological rationale and recently reported positive six-
  month data. Based on an estimated ~8,500–12,000 PWS patients with hyperphagia/
  obesity across the U.S. and EU, we conservatively model >$1B in global peak sales. We
  expect additional data by YE2026 (not formally guided) and in 2027 to further de-risk the
  opportunity, driving further upside to the stock.

SRRK INVESTMENT THESIS


●  Lead asset apitegromab addresses a key unmet need in spinal muscular atrophy (SMA),
  offering a complementary approach to SMN-targeted therapy by providing increased
  muscle function and mass. We believe Scholar Rock is positioned to deliver a strong
  commercial ramp given the expected high demand.


●  The well-established market and favorable reimbursement environment in SMA should
  enable rapid adoption of apitegromab into standard of care, and over time support a (well
  above consensus) multi-billion-dollar commercial opportunity.


●  The company is also developing apitegromab in FSHD. While we believe the program has
  a plausible scientific rationale, we continue to assign a relatively low probability of success
  given the disappointing track record of prior anti-myostatin therapies in the indication. As
  such, we attribute only modest value to the FSHD program in our valuation.


●  SRRK is well positioned to expand apitegromab (and subcutaneous follow-on SRK-439)
  into other rare muscular dystrophies. We also take partnership/BD interest in muscle
  sparing obesity applications as unmodeled upside to our valuation.

VALUATION
