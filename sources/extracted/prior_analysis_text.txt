                                                                                                                                                     Caveats
      Focused on products with more historical data; limited label expansion


      ▪   Analysis caveats:
            ▪   Data availability: analyst estimate look-back and actual sales near peak are often limited
                (esp. given prolonged 7+ year uptake to peak often seen in rare diseases)
            ▪   Excluded products (e.g., Solris) where initial estimates assumed single indications and product eventually
                expanded to additional distinct indications
            ▪   Included some indication expansion products when model is still driven by core initial indication + there
                are SYNGAP1 relevant learnings
            ▪   Also, looked at SMA market where fast followers / multiple products were in sequence in the pipeline to get
                at potential dynamics in a multiple modality DMT market*














2         * Excluded: TTR given cardio expansion and Alnylam cannibalizing its own products; Dravet seizure focused market given short sequence of Epidiolex / Fintepla launches (eventual mkt leader), also approved for LGS

=== PDF PAGE 3 ===
                                                                                                                                          Results Summary
      No clear trend; analyst over & underestimates can inform SYNGAP1


      ▪  Analysts both over and underestimate 1st entry rare disease product revenue; no consistent trend emerged

      ▪  Misses in both directions are often due to TAM and penetration estimate mismatches
            o   Specifically related to initial populations modeled (e.g., more severe, pediatric), lower initial epi estimates
                  o   Both early Strensiq and Crysvita models did not assign much value to adult mkt; limited overall
                      population literature epi; patient identification improved post-launch
            o   Highlights importance of augmenting genetic testing and patient identification pre-launch
      ▪  Geographic expansion often conferred upside vs. initial launch forecasts
      ▪  Premium pricing is frequently applied in analyst models and does not typically underly forecast misses*













3            * note: there are specific instances – e.g., weight-based dosing + under-calling adult penetration – that resulted in low initial annual price / patient assumptions (not as relevant to IT ASOs)
