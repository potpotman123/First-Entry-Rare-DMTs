What the evidence establishes

The available evidence does not establish systematic underforecasting of approval-conditional sales for first rare-disease DMTs. It supports a narrower research agenda: identify which patient-finding, uptake, persistence, delivery and geographic assumptions were wrong in specific launches, then test those mechanisms against CMP-002’s actual product profile.

This is an evidence-access conclusion, not a finding that forecasts are unbiased. The strict primary sample is N = 0 drug–indication pairs: none of the recovered historical forecasts simultaneously provides a complete matched three-year curve, documented risk treatment, territory and indication scope, and an eligible vintage. A sign test, median bias, confidence interval, leave-one-out estimate or generic sales uplift would therefore be fabricated precision.

The current CAMP reports materially sharpen the question. Needham already models $2.1bn of conditional worldwide peak sales, substantial diagnosis growth, and premium chronic pricing. Its valuation then applies 25% PoS and discounts a 2032 launch. An undervaluation argument must identify the specific commercial assumption or valuation input that is wrong; “rare-disease analysts tend to be conservative” does not do that work.

Scope: historical commercial forecasting and analogue selection, plus the supplied Street baseline. This report does not build an independent CMP-002 sales model, reverse DCF, clinical PoS estimate or price target.

Sources: needham [user PDF].

Needham’s auditable commercial assumptions

Pages 25–27 provide the most complete approval-conditional model in this packet. US launch is 2032; EU4+UK revenue starts in 2033 and is set to 25% of US sales. The model labels its 2026 US population as 11,000 patients under 18, grows it 0.5% annually, and increases diagnosis from 15% to 50% by 2040. Penetration reaches 50% of diagnosed patients in 2037. Those are separate denominators: the mature model treats roughly one-quarter of estimated prevalent patients before the adherence/persistence adjustment.

Launch annual WAC is $575,000, growing 3% per year. The model applies a 15% gross-to-net discount and a 75% combined adherence/persistence factor. Its “net annual price” is consequently $366,563 at launch: $575,000 × 85% × 75%. Economically, that is revenue per modeled patient after utilization, not simply a net drug price. Applying a separate persistence haircut to that figure would double count it.

At the 2046 peak, the model has about 3,039 US patients and $554,459 of annual revenue per modeled patient, yielding about $1.685bn US and $421m outside the US. These are broker assumptions to test, not established epidemiology, achievable price, persistence or clinical efficacy. Diagnosis growth after launch is already explicitly present.

Valuation and reconciliation points

  • Needham’s 25% PoS belongs in the valuation bridge. Its conditional sales curve must not be divided by 25% again. Its DCF and 7x peak-sales method receive equal weights, producing a printed blended equity value of $765m and approximately $8 per share on 98m diluted shares.
  • Needham p.29 uses $1.843bn in 2043 for the multiple method, while p.26 shows $1.899bn in 2043 and a $2.106bn peak in 2046. P.27’s sensitivity matrix uses $538,309, versus $554,459 in its adjacent peak summary. These are visible internal input discrepancies, not extraction errors. Reconcile the native model before replicating its target.
  • The headline $6 drug / $2 cash decomposition does not exactly match p.29’s printed $630m drug / $135m cash on 98m shares. Those figures imply approximately $6.43 and $1.38; the rounded $8 target is still consistent with the total. Preserve exact dollar values rather than relying on headline rounding.
  • Needham’s p.28 DCF expense rows differ from its p.31 operating model: for example, 2027 R&D is about $14m versus $55m and SG&A $6m versus $24m. The relationship is consistent with 25% weighting, but the model’s expense-risk methodology is not fully documented. These DCF rows are unsuitable as unconditional cash-burn assumptions.
  • William Blair’s p.4 has 2030 SYNGAP revenue as well as much larger UCD revenue. Timing, conditional status and pipeline attribution require clarification before comparing this with Needham’s 2032 launch. Leerink explicitly leaves several other opportunities unvalued. A single “Street model” would conceal these differences.

CMP-002: the context that determines relevance

As of the November 5 update, trial activities had begun and first dosing was still expected by year-end 2026. CMP-002 is a regulatory-RNA-targeting ASO intended to increase endogenous SYNGAP1 expression. Human benefit had not been demonstrated. CAMP also disclosed a SHANK3 program; the company is not literally a single-asset entity.

Sources: CAMP4 Q3 2026 update, November 5, 2026.

The September protocol disclosure describes intrathecal treatment, three doses over about three months, and exploratory seizure, sleep, motor, communication, behavior and developmental endpoints. Initial eligibility is narrower than the disease population: age 2–<18, specified protein-truncating variants in exons 5–19, and a substantial symptomatic burden. This is not an approved label. A chronic commercial dosing interval, breadth of benefit, durability and reversibility remain unknown.

Sources: CAMP4 September 28, 2026 Form 8-K, Item 8.01.

Existing care includes antiseizure treatment, dietary/device approaches where appropriate, and developmental and supportive services. Uptake would occur on top of an existing care pathway. A caregiver might accept repeated procedures for broad functional gains while responding differently to a modest seizure-only benefit; that is a scenario to test, not an observed adoption rate.

Sources: CHOP: SYNGAP1-related disorders.

Patient counts require several distinct measures: biological prevalence, pathogenic and treatment-responsive genotype prevalence, diagnosed patients, patients known to advocacy groups, label eligibility, reimbursed eligibility, and ability to receive treatment. An original census study counted 1,400 known patients worldwide, including 401 in the US, at March 31, 2024. That historical advocacy count is neither a 2026 count nor total diagnosed prevalence. Its variant-incidence analysis also shows that extrapolated missense and truncating populations need separate scrutiny.

Sources: Original SYNGAP1 patient-census and incidence research.

Needham separately cites a September 2026 global advocacy census of 1,871 and claims-based estimates from earlier company materials. Those populations and methods should not be blended into one diagnosis-rate denominator. Its physician survey comprises 16 respondents managing about 85 patients; stated willingness to use a hypothetical profile is useful qualitative evidence, not independently observed demand. The 11,000 under-18 US population in its model needs its own epidemiological validation.

Sources: needham [user PDF].

Competitive first status is provisional. Stoke’s August 2026 update places its SYNGAP1 work in lead optimization. Commercial forecasts should track future RNA and gene-therapy entrants, trial progress and time to launch, rather than assume permanent exclusivity. A late-stage Dravet ASO can inform development or administration questions, but is not yet a historical launched SYNGAP1 sales analogue.

Sources: Stoke Q2 2026 update, August 3, 2026.

Protocol, definitions and analysis pipeline

The dated protocol is supplied separately. It was written before this run’s case-level forecast-error calculations; prior familiarity with well-known cases means this is not blinded preregistration. Approval and clinical sources seed the cohort; forecast availability does not determine scientific relevance. Dated forecasts are then matched with issuer sales, and only documented transformations are permitted.

Conditional-on-launch analysis is legitimate for the commercial question. It does not establish that approved drugs represent all programs: PoS normalization does not remove selection on unobserved efficacy, label quality, commercial attractiveness or survival of coverage. The current sample also faces document-access selection: spectacular forecasts and large launches are more likely to leave public quotations.

Original broker models offer the best precision but require licensed access. Public dated quotations are fast to audit but usually omit curves and risk treatment. Original company filings offer strong recognized-sales evidence yet often cannot separate labels or territories. Claims, infusion-center records, testing laboratories, patient organizations and payer policy histories may identify mechanisms, but each observes a selected population. These sources are complements, not interchangeable proxies.

No paid data, bulk scraping or large OCR was undertaken. Several targeted leads were pursued after the initial pass; expansion stopped when original historical forecasts became the binding constraint. Official registry details could not be independently reconciled; a third-party CTIS mirror was not treated as the official record.

Protocol deviations and incomplete elements

  1. No complete FDA orphan approval export was reconciled. Ledger is a screened candidate set, not an exhaustive universe or denominator for selection adjustment.
  2. No historical record satisfies the complete 36/60-month, risk-treatment, geography and vintage requirements. Strict quantitative sample remains empty; contextual point comparisons are separate.
  3. Public quotations and one original commercial forecast publication substitute only as lower-tier contextual evidence, not as fully normalized broker models.
  4. Current CAMP reports arrived during work and were added as a separate benchmark; they did not change historical cohort rules.
  5. Machine-readable JSON replaces the preferred CSV because nested source, scope and exclusion fields are retained without flattening. Calculations are reproducible with standard Python.
  6. Trial registry mirror was encountered but not accepted as an official verified registry. September 8-K controls trial-design facts pending official protocol reconciliation.

A full approval-based census remains unfinished. Consequently the ledger cannot support a formal estimate of forecast-availability bias or a population-wide conclusion. The record-level exclusions are retained so licensed data can later extend this study without changing its rules.

Quantitative results and their limits

Strict matched 36-month cohort: N=0. Strict 60-month cohort: N=0. Formal aggregate signed bias: not estimable. The same empty-sample result applies to high-quality-only, fixed-US, early-stage and label-clean sensitivities. There is no valid three-year result to repeat in a five-year-mature subset. Case studies: what to test in each

Spinraza / SMA

Before approval: the recovered August 2016 Needham quote followed favorable pivotal interim evidence. It was not a preclinical forecast and was not consensus. Observed: the reported 2022 outcome was far below that ambitious forecast. Separately, a postapproval second-quarter 2017 consensus was beaten sharply; that cannot be relabeled a preapproval forecasting success.

Mechanisms to distinguish: fast access to known patients and loading doses can accelerate first-year sales; persistence, maintenance dosing, subsequent treatment choices and territory mix can alter later years. Company disclosures document competitive and geographic pressures, but do not assign a causal fraction of the 2016 forecast miss to each factor. A small commercial-insurance claims study also illustrates that scheduled doses need not equal observed utilization; it is not a population-wide adherence estimate.

Transfer: prioritize procedure capacity, starts versus maintained patients, and age-specific benefit. Do not transfer SMA’s clinical effect size to SYNGAP1. This case is particularly valuable because it can challenge both a “rapid launch guarantees large lifetime sales” thesis and a “intrathecal delivery prevents uptake” thesis.

Sources: Biogen: FDA approval of Spinraza, December 23, 2016; Needham forecast quoted August 1, 2016 by StreetInsider; Biogen 2022 Form 10-K, Spinraza revenues; BioWorld: Biogen second-quarter 2017 earnings; Spinraza prescribing information; Prime Therapeutics AMCP 2019 Spinraza claims poster.

Brineura / CLN2

Before approval: no usable contemporaneous sales curve was recovered. Observed: sales rose from $8.6m in the partial 2017 launch year to $128.034m in 2021. Original approval addressed slowing loss of ambulation in children aged at least three. Later age expansions must be separated.

Mechanisms to distinguish: diagnosis, referral and center activation can each limit starts. Intraventricular delivery through an implanted access device has a different burden from intrathecal ASO dosing. Low early revenue could reflect a genuinely tiny pool, slow center activation or simply a forecast that already expected both. Without the forecast and patient/center series, it is not evidence that analysts missed.

Transfer: strong analogue for pediatric CNS logistics and treatment windows; weak direct price or population analogue. Obtain time from diagnosis to referral, referral to procedure, and procedure to reimbursed treatment before raising early CMP-002 penetration.

Sources: BioMarin 2017 Form 10-K; BioMarin 2019 Form 10-K, product revenues; BioMarin 2021 Form 10-K, product revenues; Brineura FDA label, 2024.

Daybue / Rett

Before approval: Reuters quoted identifiable US peak estimates from SMBC and RBC, but not their intervening annual trajectories. Observed: reported product sales were $177.2m, $348.4m and $391.4m in 2023–2025, totaling $917.0m across the three calendar years. That is neither three full commercial years from an April launch nor a quantity comparable with one future year’s peak.

Mechanisms to distinguish: unmet need can generate initial interest while tolerability and perceived benefit affect continuation. The original label’s gastrointestinal adverse-event profile makes persistence a necessary question; it does not quantify its contribution to revenue. Caregiver reports, refill cohorts, free-drug use and payer access are needed to distinguish starts from sustained treatment.

Transfer: a high-priority neurodevelopmental adoption comparator, kept in the adjacent disease-specific cohort. Oral delivery differs from CMP-002. Do not claim proven developmental recovery or use early total-product revenue to declare that 2032/2035 US forecasts have been beaten.

Sources: Reuters March 10, 2023 report quoting preapproval SMBC and RBC notes; Acadia full-year 2024 results; Acadia full-year 2025 results; Daybue original FDA label, 2023; FDA: first treatment for Rett syndrome.

Kalydeco / G551D cystic fibrosis

Before approval: no suitable original annual curve was recovered. A readily found JPMorgan forecast is dated May 2012, after January approval, and is excluded. Observed: product sales reached $631.674m in 2015, but later genotype and age expansions make aggregate product revenue unsuitable for the original G551D population.

Mechanisms to distinguish: known genotype-positive patients, testing expansion and new label eligibility are different growth sources. A revenue beat driven by later mutations would not answer the fixed-indication/label question. Transfer: model responsive genotype and age eligibility explicitly; do not raise both prevalence and penetration for the same newly identified patient.

Sources: FDA orphan approval history for ivacaftor; AP, May 15, 2012: JPMorgan Kalydeco forecast; Vertex 2015 Form 10-K.

Strensiq / hypophosphatasia

Before approval: a September 2015 quotation gives a $355m 2018 forecast, but the public excerpt does not establish geography or PoS. Observed: worldwide revenue was $475.1m, while US revenue alone was $374.3m. Resolving geography therefore changes the apparent magnitude substantially.

Mechanisms to distinguish: finding previously undiagnosed patients, treating the known pool, geographic rollout and realized price could all contribute. A plausible patient-finding story is not a measured revenue bridge. The source’s unrelated acquisition-history error also argues for obtaining the underlying report. Transfer: useful for pediatric genetic diagnosis and chronic treatment adoption, with somatic endpoints and weight-dependent economics that differ from CNS ASOs.

Sources: FirstWord September 2, 2015, citing Bloomberg analyst estimates; Alexion 2018 Form 10-K geographic product-sales table; Strensiq prescribing information.

Onpattro / hATTR polyneuropathy

Before approval: Clarivate’s March 2018 publication gives $83m in 2018, $373m in 2019 and $1.212bn in 2022. It is an authentic dated forecast publication, but its Cortellis figures are not established as pure sell-side consensus. Observed: 2018 sales were $12.5m and 2022 sales $558m.

Mechanisms to distinguish: launch timing, infusion access, competition, geography and assumptions about cardiomyopathy could explain differences. Cardiomyopathy revenue must not be imported into a polyneuropathy test; a successor product’s sales cannot be added to rescue the original product forecast. Prior transplantation and European treatment history also complicate “first DMT” status.

Transfer: RNA modality alone does not establish comparable adoption. This is a useful counterweight to a blanket bullish thesis, pending a clean underlying forecast and original-indication allocation.

Sources: Clarivate Drugs to Watch 2018, methodology p.7 and patisiran p.23; Alnylam 2018 Form 10-K; Alnylam 2022 Form 10-K.

Skysona / CALD and Zynteglo / beta-thalassemia

Before approval: no usable annual analyst curve recovered. Observed: 2023/2024 US sales were $12.373m/$9.917m for Skysona and $16.692m/$62.273m for Zynteglo. Revenue is recognized at infusion; cell-collection guidance is not revenue. The products are kept separate from each other and from Lyfgenia.

Mechanisms to distinguish: eligibility, conditioning, collection/manufacturing and infusion capacity can separate patient interest from recognized sales. Prior transplantation means these are not strict “no previous DMT” examples. Subsequent safety evidence must be dated rather than backdated into launch-era classification.

Transfer: useful operational downside cases, not direct chronic-ASO revenue multiples and not proven analyst misses without their forecasts.

Sources: Bluebird 2024 annual report, financial statements F-20; FDA Skysona 2022 regulatory review.
