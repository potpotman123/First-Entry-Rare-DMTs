INDEPENDENT REBUILD — FINAL COVERAGE / DEVIATIONS, 9 NOVEMBER 2026

1. Source-independent, not blinded: the prior supplied cases and outcomes were already known. The FDA frame and case-priority file were saved before new historical forecast retrieval. No claim of formal preregistration. The universe builder reads only regulatory files, never the supplied deck or forecast data.
2. Retrieved frames: 1,387 CDER original approvals, 1985–2025, plus 55 products on the current CBER cell/gene roster. CDER contains 924 approvals in 2000–2025, including 380 with at least one original orphan-designated use. Partial orphan-designation strings are retained. No original orphan designation is a separate screen status, not proof that an indication is common.
3. Failed frame: OOPD request returned a search form; a subsequent session request failed. The retained HTML is a failed-access artifact, not an export. No all-orphan census is claimed. Original NME/biologic approvals omit some repurposed products, non-NMEs and first rare indications approved as supplements. Current CBER roster is not a historical census and may omit withdrawn products. CBER date verification prioritized clinically relevant products; dates are deliberately null for unadjudicated excluded rows.
4. Focused primary scope: oncology/tumor, acute infections, diagnostics and supportive/acute interventions are separately screened out. Some exclusions go beyond the original oncology-only exclusion; this is a disclosed narrowing toward chronic genetic/metabolic/CNS rare-DMT relevance, not a claim about every first rare-disease medicine. Reopen the non-orphan and non-NME strata before a population-level rare-disease bias estimate.
5. Clinical labels: every row has an explicit provisional disposition. These are not 1,442 individually verified first-DMT adjudications. Original dates/indications derive from FDA; first status, prior care and relevance are investigator judgments, upgraded only by case-level sources. Investigational exposure in a medicine's own development program does not itself disqualify a first approved DMT (Nulibry clarified accordingly). Prior routine diet, transplant or off-label disease modification is a distinct sensitivity stratum.
6. Forecast evidence: no fully matched 36-/60-month curves recovered. The January 2004 analyst publication yielded three dated annual forecasts; two additional dated press quotations yielded target-year comparisons. They are exploratory raw comparisons, not approval-conditional estimates. The 2004 cases are earlier than the 9–15-month primary window; Vimizim and Kanuma are later than that window. The priority list allowed additional regulator-derived cases, including Myozyme and Elaprase; the common 2004 source justified this bounded followup.
7. Reuse: previously verified Spinraza, Brineura and other sources may inform selected cases, explicitly marked reused. They did not select the new universe. The five newly recovered target-year records are reported separately from prior deck records.
8. Statistical stopping: no eligible primary sample, no defensible bias coefficient, confidence interval, meta-regression or calibration uplift. Report raw arithmetic, probability sensitivity and missingness, not pooled error rates. No forecasts reconstructed from peak sales, no assumed approval probabilities and no later-indication allocation invented.
9. Deliverable: standalone HTML, JSON records/dictionary, complete retrieved-frame disposition, literature/access notes and calculation checks. Spreadsheet authoring adds little while curves are missing; the original FDA workbook is preserved read-only. No paid data, bulk report scraping, large OCR, outreach or model-setting changes were undertaken.
